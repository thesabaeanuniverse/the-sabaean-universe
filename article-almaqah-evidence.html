Almaqah: Evidence, Interpretation, and Later Reconstruction

The name Almaqah is securely attested in the archaeological and epigraphic world of Saba and the northern Horn. The harder question is how far modern interpretation can go beyond those attestations.

Evidence
Inscriptions, archaeology, documented objects.
Interpretation
Scholarly readings and historical models.
Reconstruction
Project-level synthesis kept visibly separate.

What is secure

Temple dedications, inscribed cult objects, and monumental contexts provide direct evidence that Almaqah held major cultic importance. Yeha and Meqaber Gaʿewa are central northern-Horn examples.

Sirwah in ancient Saba provides an important comparative context because its Almaqah temple preserves major inscriptions and monumental sacred architecture.

Where interpretation begins

Statements about Almaqah’s precise cosmic role, planetary identity, ritual symbolism, or theological hierarchy may depend on broader comparative scholarship rather than on one direct inscription.

Terminology caution: when modern archaeologists call Almaqah a 'moon god,' the phrase should be attributed to those scholars rather than silently converted into an uncontested project conclusion.

Where HCM-364 begins

HCM-364 may compare ancient cultic evidence with reconstructed celestial structures, but it should label that comparison as reconstruction. The ancient inscription remains the source; the matrix remains the modern model.

Rule: reconstruction may organize evidence. It must not rewrite the evidence.
