From Pre-Aksumite to Aksumite: Break or Continuity?

The transition from the early first-millennium BCE political landscape to Aksum was once easier to describe as a break. Newer archaeological work makes that picture less simple.

Beta Samati changes the discussion

Excavations at Beta Samati documented occupation beginning in the Pre-Aksumite period and continuing through the Aksumite era. The excavators explicitly argue that this evidence contradicts older ideas that the Yeha region was abandoned after the Pre-Aksumite period.

This does not erase political change. Aksum developed new forms of kingship, monumental display, trade, coinage, and eventually Christian institutions. Continuity in settlement does not mean continuity in every institution.

A better question

Instead of asking whether one civilization simply replaced another, archaeology allows more precise questions: Which settlements continued? Which religious practices changed? Which technologies persisted? Which political centers gained or lost importance? How did Red Sea networks shift over time?

Interpretation: continuity is best understood as a pattern to be tested at individual sites and through specific materials, not as a claim that DʿMT and Aksum were identical political systems.

Sources and further reading
