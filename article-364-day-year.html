The 364-Day Year: What the Ancient Texts Actually Describe

A 364-day schematic year is securely documented in Second Temple Jewish calendrical traditions, including the Astronomical Book of 1 Enoch, Jubilees, and Qumran materials.

Research boundary: No currently cited ancient source directly attests the full HCM-364 architecture. This article documents the ancient 364-day calendar tradition only.

Why 364 is mathematically distinctive

A year of 364 days divides exactly into 52 weeks. It can also be organized as four quarters of 91 days. In one common form discussed in scholarship on Jubilees and Qumran, each quarter contains two 30-day months and one 31-day month.

The strength of that design is weekly regularity: the same calendar date can recur on the same weekday from year to year inside the schematic system.

The Astronomical Book

1 Enoch 72–82, commonly called the Astronomical Book or Book of Luminaries, contains numerical descriptions of solar movement, changing day and night lengths, and a 364-day year. Recent scholarship has shown that chapter 72 preserves schematic astronomical knowledge with relationships to Babylonian astronomical traditions.

Important limitation: a 364-day schematic calendar is not identical to the physical tropical year, which is about 365.24 days. Any practical long-term calendar requires a way to address the difference.

Qumran and Jubilees

Scholarly work on Jubilees and Qumran calendrical texts documents a fixed 364-day structure divided into 52 weeks and four 91-day quarters. Debate continues over antiquity, intercalation, and how such systems related to other Jewish calendars.

HCM-364 boundary: the existence of ancient 364-day calendars does not by itself prove the 13 × 7 × 4 HCM-364 matrix. The numerical overlap is a research comparison, not direct attestation.
