Ezana: Kingship, Inscriptions, and Religious Change

The reign associated with King Ezana is unusually important because royal inscriptions and coinage preserve changes in political language, religious symbolism, and public representation.

Evidence
Primary sources, archaeology, documented objects.
Interpretation
Historical arguments stated with uncertainty where needed.
Reconstruction
Kept separate from direct attestation.

The value of multilingual inscriptions

UNESCO notes that some fourth-century inscriptions of Ezana survive in Greek, Sabaean script, and Geʿez. This multilingual record gives historians a rare opportunity to compare how royal authority was communicated across different written traditions.

The presence of several scripts should not be reduced to a simple ethnic label. Administrative and monumental languages can serve political, diplomatic, religious, and prestige functions.

Religion and royal representation

Aksum adopted Christianity during the fourth century. The archaeological and numismatic record shows a change in public symbols over time, with crosses appearing on later Aksumite coins.

Evidence versus chronology: individual coins and inscriptions must be dated carefully. A ruler’s imagery can change during a reign, and different media may not change at the same moment.

Why Ezana matters

Ezana’s evidence sits at the intersection of three subjects: military kingship, multilingual inscription, and religious transformation. It is therefore one of the best case studies for showing how political history should be reconstructed from several independent source types rather than from a single text.
