Testing HCM-364: From Reconstruction to Research Program

A reconstruction becomes useful when its individual claims can be measured, compared with independent evidence, contradicted, revised, or abandoned. HCM-364 is therefore treated here as a defined research architecture that can be tested rather than as evidence for its own antiquity.

Research boundary: HCM-364 has a fixed internal architecture. Ancient inscriptions, archaeology, historical texts, calendar systems, and astronomical observations remain independent evidence. The internal consistency of HCM-364 does not by itself establish that the complete system existed historically in antiquity.
[A] Attested
Directly documented inscriptional, textual, archaeological, calendrical, linguistic, chronological, or repeatable astronomical evidence.
[B] Defensible comparative
Controlled comparison among independent bodies of evidence in which similarities, differences, chronology, and alternative explanations are all recorded.
[C] HCM reconstruction
The integrated HCM-364 architecture and proposed historical relationships extending beyond direct ancient attestation.

Start with the fixed architecture

HCM-364 is organized through three primary coordinates:

13 Celestial Houses = WHEN

7 Celestial Gates = WHAT

4 Elements = HOW

13 × 7 × 4 = 364 cells

The four directions function as an orientation overlay. They do not create another multiplying dimension in the 364-cell matrix.

Internal-canon distinction: the Houses, Gates, Elements, and their positions inside HCM-364 may be fixed as project canon while the historical antiquity of the complete integrated structure remains a separate research question.

Internal consistency is not historical proof

A model can be mathematically coherent without being historically attested.

HCM-364's internal structure therefore has to be distinguished from the external historical question:

Did ancient evidence independently preserve the same integrated system?

That question cannot be answered by the HCM framework itself.

The number 364 must not prove the model

Ancient 364-day calendrical systems are historically documented.

HCM-364 also produces the number 364.

That numerical correspondence is worth investigating, but shared arithmetic does not establish common structure, common origin, transmission, or historical identity.

The central research question

A weak question is:

“Can we find ancient material that resembles HCM-364?”

A stronger question is:

Which HCM-364 claims make independent predictions that surviving evidence or repeatable observations can support, contradict, or refine?

The reconstruction must be able to fail

If every discovery can be reinterpreted as supporting HCM-364, then the model cannot be meaningfully tested.

Test 1: the thirteen Houses

The thirteen Celestial Houses form the WHEN coordinate of HCM-364.

Historical testing should look for independent ancient evidence for a recurring thirteen-part temporal structure serving a comparable function.

securely dated calendars

inscriptions naming recurring periods

astronomical systems divided into thirteen meaningful units

ritual sequences arranged into thirteen recurring periods

evidence linking the divisions into one system

Do not count unrelated groups of thirteen

Thirteen can appear in unrelated contexts.

Thirteen objects, towers, stars, names, or divisions are not automatically evidence for the thirteen HCM Houses.

Function, chronology, context, and relationships matter as much as the number.

The Book of Luminaries is comparison, not direct proof

Test 3: the four Elements

The four Elements form the HOW coordinate of HCM-364.

Historical investigation should search for independently attested four-part structures from the relevant cultural and chronological setting.

A later philosophical system containing four elements should not be projected backward automatically.

Number, meaning, terminology, chronology, and function must all be examined.

The four directions are a separate test

HCM-364 treats North, South, East, and West as an orientation overlay rather than another factor in the multiplication.

Archaeological orientation can therefore be tested independently.

Questions might include whether monuments, sanctuaries, viewing points, or ritual spaces show repeated relationships with cardinal or seasonal directions.

Measurement rule: orientation should be surveyed rather than estimated visually.

Integration is the hardest test

The strongest evidence would demonstrate relationships among the categories themselves.

Ideally, a securely dated source or group of closely related sources would show that a thirteen-part temporal dimension, seven-part celestial dimension, and four-part structural dimension belonged to one coordinated system.

Test 5: astronomy

Some HCM propositions can be checked directly against the physical sky.

Solar positions, lunar phases, Venus visibility, planetary cycles, stellar rising and setting, daylight length, and horizon positions can all be calculated or observed.

Astronomical claims should therefore produce quantitative or observational predictions where possible.

Test 9: chronology

Sources separated by centuries or millennia should not be treated as one undifferentiated historical system.

Every proposed correspondence should include a date or date range.

Chronology rule: similarity across time can result from continuity, borrowing, revival, independent development, or coincidence. Chronology must be established before choosing among those explanations.

Test 10: historical transmission

When similar structures occur in different regions, the project should ask whether historical transmission is plausible.

Record similarities and differences equally

Confirmation bias becomes a major risk when a research archive records only matches.

Every HCM comparison should preserve contradictory evidence as carefully as supporting evidence.

What does not match?

What is missing?

What alternative explanation exists?

Contradiction is useful

Contradictory evidence does not damage the research process.

It identifies where the reconstruction may need revision.

The difference between the six-gate solar system in the Book of Luminaries and the seven canonical HCM Gates is a good example.

Preserving that disagreement is more informative than forcing the two systems into artificial agreement.

Confidence should be explicit

Not all claims deserve the same confidence.

Confidence should follow the evidence rather than the importance of a claim to the HCM framework.

Negative evidence matters

Failure to find an expected inscription, alignment, sequence, or astronomical relationship does not always disprove an ancient system.

Evidence can be destroyed or remain undiscovered.

But repeated failures across independent tests should reduce confidence in the relevant HCM proposition.

Do not move the goalposts

If a prediction fails, the original prediction should remain in the research record.

A revised hypothesis can then be created as a new version.

This prevents unsuccessful predictions from disappearing after the model is changed.

Preserve internal canon separately from historical confidence

A useful distinction is to maintain two separate records:

HCM internal definition

What the current HCM-364 model says.

Historical evidence status

How strongly surviving ancient evidence independently supports that part of the model.

This prevents future editorial changes from confusing project canon with archaeological proof.

Separate discovery from presentation

A polished diagram can make an uncertain reconstruction appear more secure than the evidence warrants.

Visual presentation should therefore preserve the evidence labels.

Readers should immediately be able to distinguish ancient evidence, measured modern observation, scholarly interpretation, and HCM reconstruction.

A useful model generates new questions

A productive reconstruction does more than collect apparent parallels.

It generates questions whose answers can be investigated independently.

What ancient 364-day calendars legitimately contribute

Ancient 364-day calendrical traditions demonstrate that 364 was historically used as a structured annual total.

What Venus legitimately contributes

Venus provides a real and highly observable celestial cycle.

Its roughly 584-day synodic period, alternating morning and evening appearances, conjunctions, and high brightness can be independently calculated and observed.

Those astronomical facts can test HCM predictions involving the canonical Morning Star Gate.

They do not by themselves establish the antiquity of that HCM assignment.

What horizon astronomy legitimately contributes

Horizon astronomy provides a repeatable method for measuring celestial movement against stable landscape features.

It can establish whether a proposed alignment physically exists.

Archaeology, chronology, and cultural evidence are then required to assess whether the relationship was ancient and intentional.

What epigraphy legitimately contributes

Epigraphy can directly attest names, titles, actions, divine references, places, numbered structures, calendrical terms, and sometimes ritual or chronological information.

This makes securely read inscriptions especially valuable for testing historical claims.

Interpretation must still remain distinguishable from the surviving characters.

HCM-364 should remain revisable

Revision in response to evidence is a strength of the research process.

The objective is not to preserve every original assumption.

The objective is to identify which parts of HCM-364 survive serious independent testing.

The research standard

1. Preserve the source.

Do not alter inscriptions, archaeology, chronology, or observations to fit the model.

2. Declare the claim.

State exactly what HCM-364 predicts.

3. Define the test.

State what evidence would support or contradict the claim.

4. Record both positive and negative results.

Do not preserve only the matches.

5. Revise when necessary.

Change the reconstruction when independent evidence requires it.

The strongest current position

HCM-364 currently functions as a defined reconstructed architecture:

13 Celestial Houses × 7 Celestial Gates × 4 Elements = 364 cells

The four directions remain an orientation overlay.

Ancient sources independently document several subjects relevant to HCM research, including 364-day calendar traditions, ancient celestial-gate language, solar horizon movement, lunar cycles, and observable planetary behavior.

Those bodies of evidence make comparison worthwhile.

They do not yet, by themselves, constitute direct attestation of the complete HCM-364 architecture as one ancient historical system.

The next stage of the project is therefore not simply to accumulate more similarities.

It is to test the architecture systematically and preserve the results.
