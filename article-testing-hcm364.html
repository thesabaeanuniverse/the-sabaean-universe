Testing HCM-364: From Reconstruction to Research Program

A reconstruction becomes more useful when its individual claims can be measured, compared with primary evidence, contradicted, revised, or abandoned. HCM-364 is therefore treated here not as proof of an ancient system, but as a structured model that can be tested.

Research boundary: HCM-364 is a reconstructed framework. Ancient inscriptions, archaeological sites, historical texts, and astronomical observations remain independent evidence. The reconstruction must be tested against those sources rather than used to prove itself.
[A] Attested
Directly documented inscriptional, textual, archaeological, calendrical, linguistic, or repeatable astronomical evidence.
[B] Defensible comparative
Structured comparison among independent bodies of evidence where similarities and differences are both recorded.
[C] HCM reconstruction
The proposed HCM-364 architecture and any historical connections not yet directly established by ancient evidence.

Start with the architecture

HCM-364 is organized around three primary dimensions:

13 Celestial Houses = WHEN

7 Celestial Gates = WHAT

4 Elements = HOW

13 × 7 × 4 = 364 cells

The four directions remain an overlay used for orientation. They are not an additional multiplying dimension in the 364-cell matrix.

The number 364 must not be allowed to prove the model

Ancient 364-day calendars are historically documented.

HCM-364 also produces the number 364.

That numerical overlap is worth investigating, but a shared number alone does not prove that two systems have a common structure, origin, or historical line of transmission.

Method rule:

numerical similarity creates a research question.

It does not create historical proof.

Separate the ancient 364-day calendar from HCM-364

Ancient Second Temple calendrical traditions preserve a 364-day schematic year.

That year can be organized into fifty-two seven-day weeks and four 91-day quarters.

These ancient structures belong to the attested evidence.

The HCM formula:

13 × 7 × 4 = 364

is a different structural arrangement.

The existence of one does not automatically establish the other.

The central research question

The useful question is not:

“Can we find ancient material that looks like HCM-364?”

A stronger question is:

“Which parts of HCM-364 make independent predictions that ancient evidence or repeatable observation can support, contradict, or refine?”

A research model must be able to fail

If every discovery can be reinterpreted as supporting the model, then the model cannot be meaningfully tested.

HCM-364 becomes stronger as a research program when specific claims are written in advance and clear failure conditions are defined.

Test 1: the 13 Houses

The thirteen Celestial Houses form the WHEN dimension of HCM-364.

To investigate whether this structure has ancient historical support, researchers should look for independent evidence for a meaningful recurring thirteen-part division.

Useful evidence could include:

• securely dated calendars

• explicit lists of thirteen temporal divisions

• inscriptions naming recurring periods

• astronomical cycles divided into thirteen units

• ritual sequences organized into thirteen recurring periods

• consistent archaeological or textual relationships among those divisions

Do not count unrelated groups of thirteen as confirmation

The number thirteen appears in many unrelated contexts.

A thirteen-object monument, thirteen stars in an illustration, or thirteen divisions in a later tradition would not automatically establish the HCM Houses.

Function matters as much as number.

Test 2: the seven Gates

The seven Celestial Gates form the WHAT dimension of HCM-364.

The project can search for ancient evidence involving seven named celestial powers, stations, gates, bodies, or other categories.

But the evidence must match more than the number seven.

A strong correspondence would require:

• seven components

• celestial function

• compatible identities

• compatible ordering

• compatible historical period

• evidence that they functioned together as one system

The Book of Luminaries is a useful comparison, not direct proof

1 Enoch's Book of Luminaries uses gate language for celestial motion.

Its solar scheme, however, describes six eastern gates and corresponding western gates.

HCM-364 uses seven Celestial Gates.

The difference is evidence too.

Comparative result:

ancient celestial gate language = relevant comparison.

identical seven-Gate architecture = not established by that text.

Test 3: the four Elements

The four Elements form the HOW dimension of HCM-364.

Historical testing should ask whether ancient sources from the relevant cultural and chronological setting independently attest a four-part structural system corresponding to the reconstructed element layer.

Later philosophical systems containing four elements should not automatically be projected backward into earlier societies.

Four directions are a separate question

HCM-364 treats the four directions as an overlay rather than part of the multiplying formula.

Archaeological orientation can therefore be tested separately.

Questions might include whether monuments, sanctuaries, observation points, or ritual spaces repeatedly relate to cardinal or seasonal directions.

A directional alignment should be measured rather than inferred visually.

Test 4: the 364-cell relationship

The complete matrix contains:

13 Houses × 7 Gates × 4 Elements = 364 possible cells

The critical historical question is whether independent ancient evidence ever shows these three dimensions functioning together.

Evidence for thirteen in one source, seven in another, and four in a third does not by itself prove a combined ancient 13 × 7 × 4 system.

Combination is the difficult part

Historical reconstruction is strongest when relationships among categories are themselves attested.

The ideal evidence would not simply preserve the numbers thirteen, seven, and four independently.

It would demonstrate that ancient users intentionally connected those categories within one coordinated system.

Test 5: astronomy

Some HCM claims can be evaluated directly against the sky.

Solar positions, lunar phases, planetary visibility, Venus cycles, stellar rising and setting, seasonal daylight, and horizon positions can be calculated and observed.

Astronomical claims should therefore generate numerical predictions.

Record the observing location

Sky geometry depends on location.

A valid field test must record:

• latitude

• longitude

• elevation

• observing direction

• local horizon profile

• date and time

• celestial target

• measurement method

Horizon predictions should be made before field interpretation

If HCM predicts that a particular celestial event corresponds to a House or seasonal transition, the expected position should be calculated before searching the landscape for a matching landmark.

This reduces the risk of selecting attractive alignments after the fact.

Preferred method:

define HCM claim
↓
calculate expected sky position
↓
conduct field observation
↓
record result
↓
evaluate without changing the original prediction

Test 6: archaeological orientation

A monument may face an astronomically interesting direction for many reasons, including coincidence.

A credible archaeoastronomical claim should consider:

• construction date

• original building phase

• measured axis

• horizon altitude

• astronomical declination

• defined observing position

• architectural function

• local topography

• alternative explanations

One alignment is weak evidence

A single monument aligned approximately with a sunrise does not establish a calendrical system.

Stronger evidence would come from repeated patterns across securely dated structures, especially when supported by inscriptions, ritual evidence, or other independent sources.

Test 7: epigraphy

Inscriptions provide one of the strongest possible routes to testing historical claims.

Searches should focus on securely published ancient texts involving:

• calendrical terminology

• celestial bodies

• months or seasons

• gates, stations, houses, or pathways

• ritual timing

• directional language

• numbered sequences

• divine or planetary terminology

The image must remain primary

The project should retain the authentic inscription photograph or verified published source whenever possible.

Transcription, restoration, transliteration, translation, and interpretation must remain separate layers.

HCM terminology must never be inserted into damaged ancient text simply because the reconstruction predicts it.

Test 8: language

Linguistic claims should be tested through securely read inscriptions, declared transliteration standards, historical grammar, and explicit comparison of forms.

Similar-looking words are not automatically historically related.

A proposed relationship becomes stronger when phonology, morphology, chronology, meaning, and attested usage support the same conclusion.

Test 9: chronology

A reconstruction can accidentally connect sources separated by centuries or millennia as though they represented one historical moment.

Every comparison should therefore include dates or date ranges.

Chronology rule:

similarity across time may indicate continuity, influence, independent development, or coincidence.

Chronology must be established before choosing among those explanations.

Test 10: transmission

When similar systems appear in different regions, the project should ask whether historical transmission is plausible.

Evidence might include:

• trade connections

• migration

• shared scribal traditions

• multilingual inscriptions

• borrowed terminology

• identifiable textual dependence

• overlapping chronology

Similarity without a transmission pathway may still be interesting, but the historical claim should remain weaker.

Test similarities and differences equally

Confirmation bias becomes a serious problem when research records only correspondences.

Every HCM comparison should maintain a contradiction column.

For every proposed parallel record:

What matches?

What does not match?

What is missing?

What alternative explanation exists?

What new evidence would change the conclusion?

Confidence should be explicit

Not all claims deserve the same level of confidence.

A practical research database can assign a status such as:

secure

probable

possible

speculative

contradicted

The label should follow the evidence rather than the importance of the claim to the larger model.

Negative evidence matters

Failure to find an expected inscription, alignment, sequence, or astronomical correspondence does not always disprove a historical system.

Ancient evidence can be lost.

But repeated failures across several independent tests should reduce confidence in the relevant part of the reconstruction.

Do not move the goalposts

If a prediction fails, the original prediction should remain in the research record.

A revised model can then be created as a new version.

This makes the development of HCM-364 transparent and prevents unsuccessful predictions from disappearing.

Version the reconstruction

HCM-364 can be treated like a developing research model.

Each major revision can document:

• previous claim

• new evidence

• reason for revision

• revised claim

• remaining uncertainty

Separate discovery from presentation

A polished diagram can make a reconstruction appear more certain than its evidence warrants.

Visual design should therefore preserve evidence labels.

A viewer should be able to distinguish an ancient inscription, a measured astronomical observation, and an HCM diagram immediately.

The model should generate new questions

A productive reconstruction does more than summarize existing material.

It generates questions that can be investigated independently.

Examples include:

Are thirteen-part temporal systems independently attested in the relevant ancient record?

Are seven celestial forces ever documented together?

Is a four-part structural category attested in the same system?

Do ancient horizon orientations repeat significantly?

Can the proposed calendar reproduce known seasonal observations?

Do inscriptions preserve terminology predicted by the model?

What ancient 364-day calendars legitimately contribute

Ancient 364-day calendrical traditions demonstrate that 364 was historically used as a structured number of days.

They provide legitimate comparative material for:

• complete seven-day weeks

• 52-week years

• four 91-day quarters

• seasonal organization

• schematic celestial time

• the relationship between calendar and religious order

What they cannot establish by themselves

They do not independently prove:

• thirteen HCM Houses

• seven HCM Gates

• four HCM Elements

• the complete 13 × 7 × 4 matrix

• a particular ancient Horn origin for the complete HCM model

What Venus legitimately contributes

Venus provides a real and highly observable celestial cycle.

Its approximately 584-day synodic period, alternating morning and evening appearances, conjunctions, and high brightness can all be independently calculated.

These astronomical facts can test HCM predictions involving the Morning Star.

They do not by themselves establish the historical meaning of the HCM Gate.

What horizon astronomy legitimately contributes

Horizon astronomy provides a repeatable method for measuring celestial movement against stable landscape features.

It can test whether a proposed alignment actually exists.

Archaeology must then determine whether the alignment is plausibly ancient and intentional.

What epigraphy legitimately contributes

Epigraphy can directly attest names, titles, actions, divine references, places, and sometimes chronological or ritual information.

It is particularly valuable because a securely read ancient text can place terminology within its historical context.

Interpretation must still be distinguished from what the inscription literally preserves.

One evidence stream should not carry the whole model

The strongest case for any reconstructed system comes from independent convergence.

text
+
archaeology
+
astronomy
+
chronology
+

+

If several independent lines of evidence converge on the same prediction, confidence increases.

HCM-364 should remain revisable

A reconstruction does not become weaker because part of it changes in response to evidence.

Revision is evidence that the research method is functioning.

The objective is not to protect every original assumption. The objective is to identify which parts of the model survive serious testing.

The research standard

1. Preserve the source.

Do not alter inscriptions or archaeological data to fit the model.

2. Declare the claim.

State exactly what HCM predicts.

3. Define the test.

Decide what evidence would support or contradict it.

4. Record both results.

Preserve matches and failures.

5. Revise the reconstruction.

Change the model when the evidence requires it.

The strongest current position

HCM-364 currently functions as a reconstructed research architecture:

13 Houses × 7 Gates × 4 Elements = 364 cells

Ancient sources independently document several subjects relevant to that reconstruction, including 364-day calendrical traditions, celestial gate language, solar horizon motion, lunar cycles, and observable planetary behavior.

Those parallels make comparison worthwhile.

They do not yet constitute direct attestation of the complete HCM-364 matrix as one ancient historical system.

The next stage is therefore not simply to add more parallels.

It is to test the architecture systematically.

[A] Attested

Primary inscriptions, archaeological contexts, securely dated texts, documented calendar systems, and repeatable astronomical observations.

[B] Defensible comparative

Structured similarities supported by independent evidence while preserving differences, chronology, and alternative explanations.

[C] HCM reconstruction

13 Celestial Houses × 7 Celestial Gates × 4 Elements = 364 cells, together with any proposed ancient integration not yet directly demonstrated by the surviving record.
