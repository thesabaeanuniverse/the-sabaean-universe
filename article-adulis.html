Adulis: The Port Connecting Aksum to the Red Sea

Adulis, in present-day Eritrea, was the principal maritime outlet of the Aksumite world and a key node linking the Horn to Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and Indian Ocean exchange.

Evidence
Primary sources, archaeology, documented objects.
Interpretation
Historical arguments stated with uncertainty where needed.
Reconstruction
Kept separate from direct attestation.

A port with a long history

Archaeological and literary evidence place Adulis within Red Sea exchange by at least the first century CE, while excavated material indicates a longer settlement history extending back into the first millennium BCE.

Research on western Indian Ocean trade identifies Adulis as the main coastal settlement of Aksum. Imported amphorae, Mediterranean marble, local ceramics, and harbor-related evidence show that the site was tied into maritime networks rather than functioning only as an inland-facing settlement.

What moved through Adulis

Ivory is repeatedly associated with the Red Sea trade of the region, but Adulis participated in a wider movement of goods, people, religious ideas, and technologies. Imported containers and building materials show contacts with ports far beyond the Horn.

Archaeological caution: the exact political status of Adulis at every point in its long history is not equally clear. Its relationship to Aksum must be dated rather than assumed to have remained identical for centuries.

Religious change at the port

Recent excavations of churches at Adulis produced radiocarbon dates in the sixth and early seventh centuries CE. The buildings show multiple phases of development, offering material evidence for Christianity in a cosmopolitan port setting.

This evidence is especially useful because it lets researchers study religious change through architecture and use of space rather than relying only on later narratives.

Sources and further reading

  2. Mark Horton & colleagues, “Archaeology of Trade in the Western Indian Ocean, 300 BC–AD 700,” Journal of Archaeological Research.
