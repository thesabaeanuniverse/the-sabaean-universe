Epigraphy

Source discipline

The inscription comes before the interpretation

Every historical argument should begin with the surviving object, its provenance, the visible signs, and the published reading before moving into restoration, translation, or wider historical reconstruction.

[A] Attested
Visible signs, original photographs, object material, findspot, archaeological context, documented provenance, and securely published readings.
[B] Interpretation
Character identification, restoration, normalization, transliteration, translation, dating, linguistic analysis, and historical explanation.
[C] Reconstruction
Proposed missing passages, historical syntheses, reconstructed relationships, and HCM-364 interpretations not directly preserved in the inscription.
Permanent visual rule: authentic supplied inscription → verified published inscription → no inscription. Generated pseudo-Musnad or decorative invented ancient script is not used as historical evidence.

Epigraphic workflow

Keep every stage visible

1. Original photograph
Preserve the source image before interpretation.

2. Diplomatic transcription
Record what can actually be read from the surviving signs.

3. Restoration
Supply damaged or missing text only when justified and mark it clearly.

4. Transliteration
Represent the ancient script using a declared scholarly system.

5. Translation
Render the text into another language while preserving uncertainty.

6. Historical interpretation
Ask what the inscription tells us about ruler, deity, event, institution, language, or chronology.

7. Reconstruction
Place broader historical or HCM-364 synthesis only after the earlier evidence layers are complete.
Source image

Photography is evidence

Preserve

Keep the archival master

Store the highest-quality unaltered image available before creating cropped, enhanced, or publication versions.

Enhance

Improve visibility, not content

Cropping, rotation, exposure, contrast, and perspective correction may improve readability without creating ancient signs that are not present.

Document

Preserve provenance

Record where the image came from, where the object was found, where it is held, and which publication or catalogue supports the identification.

Restoration

Missing text must remain visibly missing

Damage is part of the evidence.

If an ancient surface is broken, worn, or unreadable, the source image should preserve that condition.

A proposed restoration can be presented separately when supported by surviving traces, grammar, formulaic parallels, or related texts.

Restored characters must never be silently inserted into a photograph as though they survived on the original object.
Transliteration

Use a declared standard

Transliteration is a scholarly representation of the ancient writing. It is not the inscription itself. The project should therefore preserve the source image, explain the transliteration system being used, and avoid silently normalizing uncertain or damaged signs.

Original script
Reproduce verified ancient characters only when the reading and character forms are supported by the source.
Transliteration
Convert the source signs into a declared scholarly notation while marking uncertainty.
Normalization
Normalize spelling or forms only in a separate layer so the original reading remains recoverable.
Translation

Translation is interpretation

Translation requires decisions about grammar, vocabulary, titles, divine names, damaged passages, and historical context. Where more than one reading is possible, the uncertainty should remain visible rather than being hidden behind a single confident English sentence.

Direct evidence:
“This inscription preserves this name or title.”

Translation:
“This expression is translated this way under the chosen grammatical interpretation.”

Historical interpretation:
“This may indicate a political, religious, or institutional relationship.”

These are different claims and should not be merged.
Royal inscriptions

A ruler's claim is evidence of the claim

Royal inscriptions are primary historical sources, but they are not neutral reports. A victory inscription directly establishes that the ruler publicly claimed victory. Whether every detail of the event occurred as described requires comparison with independent evidence.

Quality control

Questions for every inscription

Object

Is the provenance known?

Identify the findspot, archaeological context, present location, and publication history before building historical claims from the text.

Reading

Which signs are secure?

Distinguish visible letters from damaged, uncertain, restored, or normalized forms.

Interpretation

What is being inferred?

Mark the point where the ancient wording ends and modern historical interpretation begins.

Chronology

How is the date established?

Separate secure archaeological dating from palaeographic, linguistic, stylistic, or historical estimates.

Language

Is the linguistic analysis reproducible?

Preserve the original form, declare the transliteration method, and show the grammatical basis of proposed translations.

Reconstruction

Can the claim be removed from the source?

If an HCM or broader historical claim disappears when the modern reconstruction is removed, it should remain labeled reconstruction.
