Series 01 • Complete

DʿMT & the Early Horn

Five connected articles are now published. The series moves from the evidence for DʿMT itself through Yeha, Almaqah, epigraphic method, and the question of continuity toward Aksum.

Evidence
Inscriptions, archaeological contexts, monuments, documented objects, chronology, and published excavation results.
Interpretation
Historical models concerning political organization, cultural contact, chronology, religion, and regional relationships.
Reconstruction
Broader syntheses that go beyond what individual inscriptions or archaeological contexts directly establish.
Editorial rule: authentic supplied or verified inscription imagery is preferred. Generated pseudo-script is not used as historical evidence, and reconstruction remains visibly labeled.

Research questions

What this section is testing

When?

Political organization

What was DʿMT?

What do surviving titles, settlements, monuments, and inscriptions actually permit us to say about political organization?

Regional connections

How connected was the region?

Which architectural, linguistic, material, and inscriptional links support interaction across the Red Sea and within the northern Horn?

Religion

What is directly attested?

Which divine names, sanctuaries, dedications, objects, and cultic contexts are securely documented?

Epigraphy

What do the inscriptions say?

Preserve the ancient text before moving from transcription to translation and historical interpretation.

Continuity

What changed before Aksum?

Examine continuity and transformation without forcing the evidence into either a complete-break or unchanged-continuity model.
