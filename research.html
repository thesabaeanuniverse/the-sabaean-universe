Evidence standard

Three research grades

[A] Attested
Direct support from ancient inscriptions, securely dated texts, archaeological contexts, documented objects, or repeatable astronomical observation.
[B] Defensible comparative
Comparison supported by multiple lines of evidence but not directly preserved as one complete ancient system.
[C] HCM reconstruction
The project's proposed synthesis, clearly labeled and open to modification when evidence conflicts with it.
Source hierarchy

What carries the most weight?

Primary evidence

Objects and texts

Inscriptions, excavated objects, architecture, coins, archaeological contexts, and surviving manuscripts are kept distinct from later interpretation.

Measurement

Repeatable astronomy

Solar, lunar, planetary, and horizon claims can be tested through calculation, fixed observation points, and repeat observations.

Scholarship

Attributed interpretation

Modern scholarly conclusions are identified as interpretations rather than silently rewritten as statements made by ancient sources.

Visual evidence

Authentic source imagery only

Inscription rule:

authentic supplied inscription
↓
verified published inscription
↓
no inscription

Generated pseudo-Musnad is not used as ancient evidence.

Cropping, tonal correction, contrast adjustment, and layout work may improve presentation, but they must not invent missing letter forms.
HCM-364

The reconstructed architecture

HCM-364 is maintained as a separate research model rather than silently embedded into ancient evidence.

Time / When

13 Celestial Houses

The temporal coordinate layer of the reconstructed system.

Celestial force / What

7 Celestial Gates

The celestial-force layer of the reconstructed system.

Self / How

4 Elements

Air, Fire, Earth, and Water form the four-element coordinate layer.

13 × 7 × 4 = 364 cells

The four directions are an orientation overlay only. They do not create a fourth multiplying dimension.
Historical boundary: ancient sources currently cited by the project document several relevant subjects — including 364-day calendrical traditions, celestial gate language, solar horizon motion, lunar cycles, and planetary observation — but they do not by themselves directly attest the complete 13 × 7 × 4 HCM-364 architecture.

Testing the model

Prediction before confirmation

1. State the HCM claim.

2. Define what independent evidence could test it.

3. Make the prediction before examining the result.

4. Record support, contradiction, and uncertainty.

5. Preserve alternative explanations.

6. Revise the reconstruction when the evidence requires it.
